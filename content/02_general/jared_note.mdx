To Whom it may concern,

I’m Jared, the Club Operations Lead at Hack Club. My role at this non-profit is to support teenagers around the world as they establish, plan, and facilitate coding clubs in their local communities. In this email, I’ll share what Hack Club is, how we support coding clubs worldwide, and how we achieve this ambitious goal.

**First, what is Hack Club?** We are a US non-profit aiming to provide opportunities for teenagers learning and developing their coding skills by working with a global community of like-minded people. Here is our [philosophy](http://hackclub.com/philosophy) page to help explain our motivations and beliefs. Basically, we are a community of ‘hackers’. But this shouldn’t be confused with the illegal form of hacking we all see in movies. Our definition of a ‘hacker’ is someone who solves problems efficiently by developing creative coding solutions. Through our philosophy, you’ll also see we believe the best way to learn coding is through building. We aim to provide these learning opportunities through various structured projects developed by fellow teenage coders. Here is a Wall Street Journal [article](https://www.wsj.com/articles/teen-hackers-try-to-convince-parents-they-are-up-to-good-11569922200) discussing this topic further.

**How do we help teens learn this ‘superpower’ of coding?** Through a type of program we call ‘You Ship We Ship’ (YSWS). Basically, it is what it says. You (a teenager) ship Hack Club a completed technical project, we ship a reward to incentivize completing it. These are often themed and structured activities we provide and can be seen at the top of our [Toolbox](http://toolbox.hackclub.com) webpage. For example, [Boba Drops](http://boba.hackclub.com) is our entry-level program for beginners to learn how to code their first website. When they ‘ship’ this website to us, we provide funding so they can purchase a Boba Tea as a reward (or so a club leader can purchase their club Boba Tea). Another example can be seen with [Sprig](http://sprig.hackclub.com), where teenagers will code a Javascript game, ‘ship’ it to us, and we send our custom Sprig handheld video game controller to upload and play it on. Basically, these incentivize learning and finishing projects by offering rewards.

**How does Hack Club provide these opportunities?** Where does the money come from? As a nonprofit with 501(c)(3) status, we are required to disclose certain financial information to the public. Anyone can check this out through [HCB](https://hcb.hackclub.com/hq/transactions)\! GitHub (the top web based platform for developers to store, manage, and share their code) is a partner of Hack Club and recently donated $250,000. You can type their name into the search and see for yourself. Another major contributor is Michael Dell, the CEO and founder of Dell Technologies, the popular PC producer.
